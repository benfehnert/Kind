For individuals

Kind is a health exploration platform — not a tracking app, a fitness app, or a supplement store. Kind matches you to structured, time-limited explorations of everyday health choices (like a morning routine for afternoon energy, or less screen time before bed for better sleep), helps you log what you do, and shows you at the end how the change related to your results. What makes Kind different is that it is science-led and ethics-covered from the outset. Every exploration is reviewed by Kind's Protocol Review Board and approved by an independent Institutional Review Board (IRB) before it goes live, and is designed to international standards for personalized (N-of-1) trials. And if you choose to, you can contribute your de-identified results to research — so exploring your own health also helps build the evidence for others.

Kind will launch in the US with explorations across a range of health categories. Examples of explorations planned for launch include: Energy & Focus (Morning Rules for Afternoon Energy); Metabolic Health (Time-Restricted Eating for Energy); Rest & Sleep (Screen Exposure Moderation for Sleep); Diet & Nutrition (Ultra-Processed Food Reduction for Energy); Mental Health (Digital Boundaries: Social Media Limit); Movement & Recovery (Cold Exposure for Recovery); Women's Health (Cycle-Synced Exercise Intensity for Strength); Skin & Dermatology (Retinoid Introduction for Skin Texture); Men's Health (Resistance Training for Energy and Mood); and Allergy & Respiratory (Breathing Technique for Breathlessness). At launch, all explorations focus on general wellness. Medication and treatment comparisons are not included at launch. New explorations are co-developed with researchers, and each one is reviewed and approved before it is added, so the library will grow over time. If there's an area you'd like to see covered, tell us when you join the waitlist.

Kind's AI companion is designed to be helpful without being demanding. It sends gentle reminders to log what you did and how you're feeling — typically once or twice a day during an active exploration. Check-ins are conversational and quick, usually taking less than a minute. The companion is there to help you follow your exploration as designed and keep track of your progress; it doesn't give medical advice or tell you what to change. You can adjust the frequency of reminders or pause them at any time.

Using Kind and contributing to research are separate choices: research use is its own opt-in, never bundled into the consent you give to use the app, and you'll be asked for specific consent for each exploration. If you opt in, your data is de-identified before researchers can use it. We're transparent about exactly how your data is used and what the research shows.

Kind is for adults aged 18 and over. Each exploration has clear eligibility criteria, and you'll only be offered explorations you're eligible for. Every exploration on Kind goes through two independent checks before anyone can take part. First, Kind's Protocol Review Board, chaired by our Chief Science Officer, reviews its scientific design and carries out a clinical and user-safety risk assessment. It is then reviewed and approved by an independent, US-based Institutional Review Board (IRB). During an exploration you can report a concern at any time in the app. If something sounds urgent, Kind will direct you straight to emergency services (911) or the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

When you join Kind and choose an exploration, you'll become part of the group for that exploration, seeing how others are progressing and sharing your own updates. You can also join topic-based communities (like the Sleep group or the Energy group) to connect with people exploring related areas, and see de-identified group results alongside your own. Sharing with external networks is optional and always under your control. The Kind community is moderated to ensure it stays supportive, kind, and evidence-focused.

For researchers

Kind is built for personalized (N-of-1) trials: structured explorations in which each individual compares conditions over time and receives their own result, and which can then be combined across many individuals. Each exploration uses one of a set of established single-case designs, such as alternating treatments, ABAB reversal, changing-criterion or elimination–reintroduction, chosen to suit the intervention. Protocols follow SPENT and SPIRIT guidance and are reported to CENT and CONSORT standards. You can co-develop explorations with Kind, analyze de-identified data from consenting participants using pre-specified analysis plans or explicit emulative analysis, and publish protocols and findings as preprints. At launch, Kind covers general wellness explorations only: behavior, sleep, diet, exercise and over-the-counter supplements. It is not designed for clinical trials of medicines or medical devices. If you're unsure whether your research question is a good fit, get in touch and we'll discuss it.

Ethics and IRB coverage are built in. Every exploration on Kind is reviewed by Kind's Protocol Review Board (a documented scientific, clinical and user-safety review against approved templates) and then approved by an independent, US-based Institutional Review Board (IRB), under Kind's umbrella protocol. Kind applies Common Rule standards voluntarily. Consent is layered: participants give separate, unbundled consent for research, Broad Consent for secondary analysis, and specific consent for each exploration. Participants can withdraw research consent at any time. Their data is then excluded from future analyses, although data already used in completed analyses can't be retrieved. If your own institution requires its own approval or a reliance agreement, we'll work with you to provide the documentation you need.

Researchers can access de-identified data from participants who have explicitly opted in to research. This includes exploration adherence and self-reported outcome measures. Wearable integration isn't planned for launch but is on our roadmap; once available, it will add any tracking data participants choose to link. Data is de-identified to the HIPAA Safe Harbor standard, with minimum group sizes. By default, researchers work with aggregate outputs; row-level de-identified data is available only under a Data Use Agreement that prohibits re-identification and onward transfer. A limited set of open, de-identified data will be available to all researchers free of charge, with full data access through a researcher subscription and priority access to the explorations you co-develop. Kind's researcher dashboard will provide tools to explore, analyze and visualize the data.

Kind is free to use for as long as you like, including access to a limited set of open, de-identified data. You can unlock more features and content by subscribing, including full data access, Kind's publication tools, and the ability to co-create explorations with Kind. Pricing hasn't been finalized and will be structured to be accessible for academic researchers. Researchers who join the waitlist ahead of launch may have the opportunity to work with us as early partners and help shape pricing and terms.

Once your exploration has been approved and published on Kind, it is offered to individuals who meet its eligibility criteria. Matching uses clear, rule-based eligibility filters, such as age range and exclusion criteria, applied at intake. Individuals choose whether to take part; they're never enrolled in an exploration without their knowledge and explicit consent. Because Kind's members are already motivated to explore their health, Kind is designed to make recruitment faster and more affordable than traditional approaches, particularly for lifestyle and supplement interventions where motivation matters for adherence.

Yes. Supporting publishable research is one of Kind's core objectives. Protocols and findings are published as preprints, with researcher names and institutions credited in a transparent and citable way, and can go on to be submitted for peer review. Group analyses follow pre-specified analysis plans, using Bayesian and inferential methods, and are reported to CENT and CONSORT standards. The strength of evidence will vary by study design and sample size, and we're committed to honest representation of what the data does and doesn't show. Kind's Science Advisory Board helps keep our analysis and publication standards rigorous.

Kind's science is guided by a Science Advisory Board of leading scientists, with expertise in personalized (N-of-1) trial design, statistical analysis, reporting standards and shared decision-making, including authors of the international guidelines for N-of-1 trials. Further specialists are invited according to the topic. The board advises on science strategy, N-of-1 design, statistical analysis and reporting standards. It doesn't approve explorations; that is the role of the Protocol Review Board and the independent IRB. We'll share further details about the board before launch. If you're a specialist who'd like to be involved, please get in touch via the researcher waitlist.

Existing platforms like ResearchMatch or Evidation focus primarily on recruiting participants for traditional clinical trials. Kind is different in several ways. Participants are already engaged in exploring their health before a researcher reaches them. Explorations are structured personalized (N-of-1) trials carried out in everyday life, so each person gets their own result and the pooled data shows how responses vary across people. Ethics (IRB) coverage applies across all activity on Kind, and individuals benefit directly from the research they contribute to. Kind is also a community, not just a data platform: researchers build a presence and reputation, not just a dataset. It's best understood as a complement to traditional research methods, not a replacement.

Kind launches as a general wellness and research platform. Its launch scope is designed to sit outside FDA medical device oversight, in line with FDA's General Wellness and Clinical Decision Support guidance: it gives no diagnosis or treatment advice and includes no medication or treatment comparisons. Kind is not a HIPAA covered entity. It follows the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and applicable state health data laws, never sells individual data, and never uses health data for advertising.

We're building toward a US launch in 2027, starting with a soft launch in early 2027, followed by a full launch at which we plan to publish the first protocols and findings. We're recruiting research partners now to co-design the researcher experience, contribute the first wave of explorations, and have their work featured as the community grows. If you join the researcher waitlist, we'll be in touch to discuss how you could be involved.
