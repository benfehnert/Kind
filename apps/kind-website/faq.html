For individuals

Kind is a health exploration platform — not a tracking app, a fitness app, or a supplement store. The key difference is that Kind helps you run structured, time-limited explorations of health interventions (like a caffeine window for sleep, or creatine for energy) and gives you personal results at the end. Unlike most health apps that just log data, Kind connects your exploration to real research and a community of people on similar journeys. It's also built in partnership with health researchers, so the protocols you follow are evidence-backed — not just trending on social media.

Kind launches with explorations across sleep, energy and focus, diet and gut health, mental wellbeing, and cardiovascular and metabolic health. Examples include: a caffeine window exploration to improve sleep quality, creatine supplementation for energy and cognitive performance, intermittent fasting protocols, HRV-guided training, screen time reduction for mood, and more. As researchers contribute new protocols and the community grows, the library will expand significantly. If there's an area you'd like to see covered, tell us when you join the waitlist.

No. Kind is designed to work without any wearables or special equipment — just your phone and your own observations. That said, if you do use a fitness tracker, smartwatch, or sleep monitor, Kind can incorporate that data to enrich your exploration results. We support data from popular devices and trackers, but it's entirely optional. The most important data is what you log yourself: how you feel, what you did, and how well you slept.

Kind's AI companion is designed to be helpful without being demanding. It sends gentle reminders to log your activity and how you're feeling — typically once or twice a day during an active exploration. The check-ins are conversational and quick, usually taking less than a minute. The companion also proactively surfaces relevant insights, celebrates your progress, and helps you troubleshoot if you're finding an exploration difficult. You can adjust the frequency of reminders or pause them at any time.

It depends on the intervention, but most explorations run for between two and eight weeks. This is long enough to see meaningful changes in how you feel and perform, but short enough to stay motivated. Some explorations include a baseline period before the intervention begins, so you have a comparison point. At the end of the exploration, Kind generates a personal summary of your results — what changed, what didn't, and how your experience compares to others who completed the same exploration.

Your health data is stored securely in your personal data vault and belongs to you. Kind never sells your data to third parties. If you choose to contribute anonymised data to research, you do so with full, explicit consent — and you can withdraw that consent at any time. When data is shared with researchers, it is always anonymised and aggregated so you are never identifiable. Kind is built to HIPAA-compliant standards and follows cybersecurity best practices throughout. We're also transparent about exactly how your data is used and who can access it.

Kind is not a medical service and does not provide medical advice. Before starting any health exploration — especially if you have a diagnosed condition or take prescribed medication — you should consult your doctor or a qualified healthcare professional. Some explorations on Kind may not be suitable for people with certain conditions, and eligibility criteria will be clearly stated for each exploration. Kind is designed to complement, not replace, professional medical care.

When you join Kind and choose an exploration, you'll automatically become part of the group for that exploration — seeing how others are progressing and sharing your own updates. You can also join topic-based communities (like the Sleep group or the Energy group) to connect with people exploring different but related interventions. Sharing with external networks is optional and always under your control. The Kind community is moderated to ensure it stays supportive, kind, and evidence-focused.

For researchers

Kind supports real-world, observational, and intervention-based explorations across a range of health domains. You can design structured protocols that individuals follow and track, gather anonymised data from consenting participants, run retrospective analyses across existing platform data, and publish pre-print findings associated with your name and institution. Kind is not designed for randomised controlled trials (RCTs) requiring clinical oversight, but it is well-suited to naturalistic studies, N-of-1 designs, citizen science explorations, and longitudinal observational work. If you're unsure whether your research question is a good fit, get in touch and we'll discuss it.

Ethical design is central to Kind. All participants provide explicit, granular consent before their data is used for research — covering what data is shared, with whom, and for what purpose. Participants can withdraw consent at any time and their data will be removed from future analysis. Kind's data sharing model is designed to meet the standards expected by academic institutional review boards (IRBs) and ethics committees, though researchers are responsible for obtaining any additional institutional ethics approval required for their specific study. We work with you to ensure that consent language is clear, honest, and specific.

Researchers access anonymised, aggregated data from participants who have explicitly consented to share their data for research purposes. This includes exploration adherence data, self-reported outcome measures, and any tracking data participants have chosen to link (such as wearable data). Individual-level data is never identifiable. The specific data available will depend on the exploration design and what participants have consented to share. Kind's researcher dashboard provides tools to query, visualise, and export data in formats suitable for analysis.

A researcher subscription will be required to create explorations, access participant data, and use Kind's publication tools. Pricing hasn't been finalised yet and will be structured to be accessible for academic researchers while reflecting the value of the platform. During the early partnership phase (before public launch), we're working with a small group of research partners on a collaborative basis — if you join the waitlist now and are accepted as an early partner, you'll have the opportunity to shape pricing and terms. Content contribution and basic knowledge-sharing features may be available at lower cost or free for qualifying institutions.

Once your exploration is published on Kind, eligible individuals are surfaced it through the AI matching system based on their profile and goals. You can define eligibility criteria (e.g. age range, health profile, existing conditions to exclude) and the platform will match participants accordingly. Individuals choose to opt in — they're never assigned to a study without their knowledge and explicit consent. Kind's community of engaged health explorers means recruitment is significantly faster and more cost-effective than traditional approaches, particularly for lifestyle and supplementation interventions where motivation is key to adherence.

Yes — one of Kind's core objectives is to support the generation of publishable research outputs. Kind provides tools to generate pre-print publications from aggregated exploration data, and researcher names and institutions are associated with findings in a transparent and citable way. The strength of Kind-derived evidence will vary by study design and sample size, and we're committed to honest representation of what the data does and doesn't show. We're working with academic advisors to ensure our analysis and publication standards are rigorous and respected.

Kind's advisory board spans data science and AI, citizen research methodologies, psychology and mental health, gut health and nutrition, cardiology and cardiovascular health, metabolic health and endocrinology, and other specialisms that will be added as the platform grows. The advisory board provides expertise to inform platform design, exploration protocols, scientific standards, and community guidelines — ensuring Kind remains grounded in evidence and best practice. If you're a specialist who'd like to be involved, please get in touch via the researcher waitlist.

Existing platforms like ResearchMatch or Evidation focus primarily on participant recruitment for traditional clinical trials. Kind is different in several ways: participants are already engaged in self-directed health exploration before a researcher ever reaches them; the platform is designed for real-world naturalistic data rather than controlled clinical settings; and Kind creates a two-way relationship where individuals benefit directly from the research they contribute to. Kind is also a content and community platform, not just a data platform — researchers build a presence and reputation, not just a dataset. It's best understood as a complement to traditional research methods, not a replacement.

We're actively building the platform and recruiting research partners ahead of the public beta, planned for late 2026. Early research partners will have the opportunity to co-design the researcher experience, contribute the first wave of explorations, and have their content and protocols featured prominently as the community grows. If you join the researcher waitlist, we'll be in touch to discuss how you could be involved in the early partnership phase — which will run from Q3 2026 ahead of the broader beta launch.
